# Design and audience

## Audience and intent — write before generation
- Intended audience: **My mother first.** Then my family and friends who know the story, watching on their phones.
- Where/how they will watch: **on a phone, upright.** The talk is Amharic, and the subtitles are English.
- What viewers should feel or understand: **She should laugh, recognise us both, and feel that this small morning is remembered with love.**

These three lines were drafted by the coding agent on 25 Nov 2026 from what the film-maker said ("the story is about me and my mom"). He kept them unchanged in the storyboard he wrote himself on 26 Nov 2026.

## Story and shot rationale

The story is the film-maker's own, in his own words, told in the first person. He was six. They lived in a rural area with two dairy cows, and his mother milked them early every day. He loved watching her and drinking the fresh milk. One morning she filled a gourd and set it by the door. He ran home after playing with friends, did not see the gourd, and knocked it over with his foot. Every drop spilled. His mother always says something when she is startled, and that day she shouted "Jesus of Nazareth!". She checked that he was not hurt, then took a small stick and came toward him. He ran as he had never run, and even the cows watched. The milk was lost, his mother said her usual proverb, and it makes them laugh to this day.

The pictures are the film-maker's own, made with an image tool of his choice. He gave twelve pictures for twelve shots and a line for each. The speech service allows 10 requests a day, so four pairs of shots were merged. Every sentence is kept, in his order and his spelling.

## How the length is decided

The narration sets the pace. Each shot stays for its planned time, or longer when its line needs it. The planned times add up to 85 s, and with the 3-second end card the film is 88 s. The film-maker's text is 617 letters, about 58 s of speech. The pipeline refuses to assemble a film outside 60 to 90 s, checked after the lines have been spoken.

## What was changed in the pictures, and why

Only cropping, to remove what contradicted the story:

- **Shot 01, first picture:** the right 8 per cent is cut, where a third cow was cut off by the edge. The story has two cows.
- **Shot 01, second picture, and shot 04, first picture:** the bottom is cut, where the gourd was already lying with milk on the ground before the accident.

Known and not corrected: the milking picture (shot 02) shows three cows.

## Shapes

Ten pictures are upright and three (the chase) are wide. The films are 9:16 and 16:9. A picture much taller or wider than the frame is looked at through a window of the frame's shape that moves slowly across it. The three wide pictures are therefore a little soft in the upright film, because a small part of each is enlarged. Upright versions of them would fix that.

## What the first version got wrong, in the film-maker's words

The first pictures were made by a small free image model from photographs. He said they looked like "a white mom and kid", that the round thatched hut was "the worst" because his home has a corrugated iron roof, and that the milk should be in a gourd. The narrator laughed all the time and the wording was not his. He then made the pictures and wrote the narration himself. This is the first and largest lesson of the design: the film-maker's own account of a place and a person beats any description an agent can invent.
